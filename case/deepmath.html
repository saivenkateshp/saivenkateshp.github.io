✍️ DeepMath

Reading a handwritten arithmetic expression from a photo and computing the answer, using classic computer vision for segmentation and a CNN for recognition.

01Problem

Turning handwriting into something a computer can calculate takes two steps: finding where each symbol is, and recognizing what it is. The goal was an end-to-end pipeline where a photo of an expression such as 157914 × 13 goes in and the computed answer comes out.

02Architecture

LayerConfiguration

Conv2D → MaxPool32 filters, 3×3, ReLU · 2×2 pool

Output20 units, softmax: digits 0–9, + − × ÷, ( ) [ ] { }

Trained with Adam and categorical cross-entropy for 15 epochs on 150,764 images, validated on 37,681 (80/20 split), with shear and zoom augmentation.

03Key decisions & trade-offs

Contour segmentation before classification

Why: Splitting the image into symbols with OpenCV turns a hard sequence problem into 20-class image classification, which is easy to train, debug and inspect symbol by symbol.

Trade-off: It assumes symbols don't touch and sit on one line, so fractions, exponents and joined handwriting break it.

A small CNN instead of a deep or pre-trained network

Why: Twenty simple glyph classes at 32×32 don't need a large model. About 160K parameters trains quickly on Colab and reached 97.5% validation accuracy.

Trade-off: There's little headroom for messier handwriting or more symbol classes.

Augmentation (shear, zoom)

Why: Handwriting varies in slant and size; augmentation makes the model robust to that without collecting more data.

Trade-off: Validation accuracy (97.5%) ended slightly above training (96.6%), most likely because augmentation and dropout make training harder than validation, so the training curve understates the model.

04Results

After 15 epochs the CNN reached 97.5% validation accuracy (96.6% training, validation loss 0.078). End to end, the pipeline read the handwritten expression 157914*13 and returned 2052882, which is correct.

05What I'd do differently

  • Report a per-class confusion matrix; look-alike pairs such as 1 / ( / ] and × / + are where errors concentrate.
  • Measure accuracy on whole expressions, not just single symbols, because one wrong symbol makes the answer wrong.
  • Move to a sequence model (CRNN with CTC loss) to handle touching symbols, multiple lines, fractions and exponents.
  • Export to TensorFlow.js for a live draw-and-solve demo in the browser, and evaluate with a small expression parser that validates the recognized string.
